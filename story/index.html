Why I built Chat Preserver

When I first started using ChatGPT, I was amazed at what it could do. I had a project in mind, and for three weeks I worked on it back and forth with ChatGPT: documenting things, uploading files, and getting files it made for me. We had a plan, and we were doing great.

Then it started asking me questions it should have known the answers to. It was running out of context, though I didn't know that word yet. Eventually it stopped responding altogether. "Something went wrong. Try again." Trying again never worked.

That's when I learned how temporary AI memory really is. All that work was trapped in a chat I could only get back to by scrolling all the way to the beginning. And forgetting wasn't the worst of it: after a while, some of the files started to disappear.

So I set out to fix it, and I figured the best tool to save ChatGPT was ChatGPT. It took months of testing, trying this and trying that, and learning which questions to ask to get the answers I needed, plus a switch to a different AI along the way.

The first win was simple: a clean Markdown copy of the conversation. I could start a new chat, upload that file, and pick up right where I left off. But text wasn't enough. I needed everything in the chat, especially the files ChatGPT had made for me that I couldn't recreate anywhere else.

So the work continued. It was refined, reworked, and refined again. Chat Preserver now saves a whole chat, or a whole project, with every file it can still reach, to your own computer. It tells you plainly what came back and what didn't, so nothing goes missing without you knowing.

And because a folder of files isn't a conversation, it also builds an offline reader: one page that opens in any browser, with search, a minimap down the side to jump around even the longest chat, and every image and file right where it appeared.

Now we're about to launch, and that makes me happy. I hope it saves your work the way it saved mine.

James, founder of Chat Preserver
